GTFO

Get The Flock Out : Flock Surveillance Accountability

I'm tired of it. Maybe you are, too.

For me, and seemingly for many in the United States, Flock cameras are the last straw. As a Millennial, I've grown up on the internet and seen companies like Google, Meta, and Amazon explode in scale and invade our lives. More and more of our data is harvested for God-knows-what reasons, which it's later uncovered are reasons not so salutary, laudable, or good. All this data harvesting seems to be good for is enriching the asset-owning class while telling us all stories amounting to "don't worry, it'll eventually be good for you! Really!"

As the father of a soon-to-be second grader, and as a professional Data Analyst, I want to do something so my daughter and her friends can enjoy learning, growing, and playing without their data being harvested to manipulate and nudge them toward ends they might not really want. But of course, on my way to drop my daughter off at school what do I drive past?

Flock cameras.

Not only are companies profiling my daughter via school devices and home devices; they now can add geographical travel patterns to their ever-growing dataset. Harvest enough data and the inferences to be drawn are significant. Be a multi-billion dollar company with scalable compute infrastructure to run machine learning models on that dataset, and that's straight dangerous.

I've grown up in an economic landscape that said work hard and it'll pay off. I did work hard and those companies decided they didn't want to make it pay off. Instead, they want to extract *even more* by gathering data on our every action, both online and now out in the world on the road. I want some control back in my life, and I want my daughter and her friends to be able to be left alone by these companies, to not constantly be observed and tracked and profiled and monitored. They say it's for safety. But the only danger I feel is from these companies and what they are free to do with their power and all this data on us.

I don't trust them because they have proven they cannot be trusted, at least in their current forms and extractive business practices.

So they can GTFO

Let's do something.

There are great orgs doing good work to empower the public around ALPRs and Flock. Deflock and MN Privacy Project (for my local Minnesotans) are two.

This project is to encourage one specific and simple action you can take to peer into how these systems are actually being used.

Here's what you can do.

Go to your city/municpality website, or your county website, and find how to submit a records request.

When you find that, request the following:

"Hi, I'd like the Flock Security event/activity log from the earliest possible date to today. I'd like this file in CSV format."

This file will contain all actions taken within the Flock system. Logging in, viewing cameras, usernames, it's all in there. This will allow you to see how your public servants (and possibly Flock employees) have been using the system.

Some concerning activities have already begun to be uncovered in other parts of the United States.
